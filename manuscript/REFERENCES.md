## Policy and the 65% rule

1. **Environment Canada (2011).** *Scientific Assessment to Inform the Identification of
   Critical Habitat for Woodland Caribou (Rangifer tarandus caribou), Boreal Population, in
   Canada: 2011 Update.* Ottawa. 102 pp. + appendices.
   - Supports: the disturbance-recruitment relationship. "Nearly 70% of the variation in
     caribou recruitment ... was explained by a single composite measure of total
     disturbance (fire + buffered anthropogenic)." Probabilities of self-sustaining
     populations "from greater or equal to 60 to 90%, corresponding to a total range-level
     disturbance values from 10 to 35%" (§3.3). Assessed 57 ranges.
   - Does **not** state "65% undisturbed = 60% probability": cite 2 for that.

2. **Environment Canada (2012).** *Recovery Strategy for the Woodland Caribou (Rangifer
   tarandus caribou), Boreal population, in Canada.* Species at Risk Act Recovery Strategy
   Series. Ottawa. xi + 138 pp.
   **ECCC (2020).** *Amended Recovery Strategy for the Woodland Caribou (Rangifer tarandus
   caribou), Boreal Population, in Canada.* SARA Recovery Strategy Series. Ottawa. xiii + 143
   pp. Cat. En3-4/140-2020E-PDF. ISBN 9780660353234. (Cite this final 2020 record; the 2019
   document was the proposed amendment.)
   - Supports: "identifies 65% undisturbed habitat in a range as the disturbance management
     threshold", which "provides a measurable probability (60%) for a local population to be
     self-sustaining"; disturbance = fire in the past 40 years + "application of a 500 m
     buffer to mapped anthropogenic features" (§4.2.1).
   - SK1: "40% undisturbed habitat in the range as the disturbance management threshold"
     (71% probability), because "SK1 is the first local population that is currently
     self-sustaining below the 65% undisturbed habitat threshold", with "high fire and very
     low anthropogenic disturbance". Anthropogenic disturbance above 5% is a destruction
     factor in SK1.

3. **ECCC (2024).** *Report on the Progress of Recovery Strategy Implementation for the
   Woodland Caribou (Rangifer tarandus caribou), Boreal population, in Canada for the
   Period 2017-2022.* Species at Risk Public Registry. (Figure 2 data in `reference/`.)
   - Supports: "21 ranges meet or exceed the undisturbed habitat threshold". The data year
     is stated in ECCC's news release of 2024-05-10: "Based on 2020 data, 21 of the total
     51 Boreal Caribou ranges meet or exceed the minimum threshold of 65 percent".

4. **ECCC (2024).** *2020 - Anthropogenic disturbance footprint within boreal caribou ranges
   across Canada - As interpreted from 2020 Landsat satellite imagery.* Open Government
   Portal, record 63e1cda6-debe-4b9b-b075-3666443e30b4. Published 2024-07-15.
   - Supports: features "buffered by a 500-metre radius"; "forest fire polygons for the past
     forty years (CNFDB 1981-2020) were merged"; "addition of CFS harvest polygons (Ref:
     NRCan-CFS NTEMS; Wulder 2020)"; "features were not removed in the mapping process"
     (old cutblocks stay in the footprint).

## Ecology: does fire count?

5. **Johnson, C.A., Sutherland, G.D., Neave, E., Leblond, M., Kirby, P., Superbie, C., &
   McLoughlin, P.D. (2020).** Science to inform policy: linking population dynamics to
   habitat for a threatened species in Canada. *Journal of Applied Ecology*, 57(7),
   1314-1327. https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2664.13637 *(Crossref)*
   - Supports: the "negative effect of fire was three to four times smaller than human
     disturbances"; the top adult-female survival model "included human disturbances only";
     65% "may serve as a reasonable proxy ... in landscapes dominated by human
     disturbances". Little Smoky needs at least 68%; SK1 was self-sustaining at 40%.

6. **Konkolics, S., Dickie, M., Serrouya, R., Hervieux, D., & Boutin, S. (2021).** A burning
   question: what are the implications of forest fires for woodland caribou? *Journal of
   Wildlife Management*, 85(8), 1685-1698. https://doi.org/10.1002/jwmg.22111 *(Crossref)*
   - Supports: "Caribou avoided burns and unburned residual patches. Increased use of burned
     habitats, however, did not lower the survival of adult caribou." 201 caribou, six
     Alberta populations (do not attribute it to the Richardson range alone).

7. **Skatter, H.G., et al. (2017).** *Canadian Journal of Zoology*, 95(12), 975-984.
   https://doi.org/10.1139/cjz-2016-0307 *(Crossref)*. In SK1: "woodland caribou preferred
   nonburned (>40 years) over burned habitats (≤40 years)"; residual patches in burns were
   preferred for calving.

8. **Dalerum, F., Boutin, S., & Dunford, J.S. (2007).** *Canadian Journal of Zoology*, 85(1),
   26-32. https://doi.org/10.1139/z06-186 *(Crossref)*. Alberta: "no change in home range
   sizes" after fire; no effect of fire on mortality or fecundity.

9. **Joly, K., et al. (2003).** *Canadian Journal of Zoology*, 81(7), 1192-1201.
   https://doi.org/10.1139/z03-109 *(Crossref)*. Alaska: "Caribou used recently burned areas
   much less than expected."

## Fire data

10. **Canadian Forest Service (2021).** *Canadian National Fire Database - Agency Fire
    Data.* Natural Resources Canada, Canadian Forest Service, Northern Forestry Centre,
    Edmonton. https://cwfis.cfs.nrcan.gc.ca/ha/nfdb
    - "a collection of forest fire locations and fire perimeters as provided by Canadian fire
      management agencies including provinces, territories, and Parks Canada." (Use this,
      not Stocks et al., which describes the older Large Fire Database.)

11. **Hall, R.J., Skakun, R.S., Metsaranta, J.M., et al. (2020).** Generating annual
    estimates of forest fire disturbance in Canada: the National Burned Area Composite.
    *International Journal of Wildland Fire*, 29(10), 878-891.
    https://doi.org/10.1071/WF19201
    - Supports: NBAC combines Landsat-based burned-area polygons and "high-quality agency
      polygons", with coarser HANDS mapping filling gaps. NBAC version used: 20260513.
    - Does **not** say NBAC excludes unburned islands: do not claim it.

12. **Skakun, R.S., et al. (2022).** *Remote Sensing*, 14(13), 3050.
    https://doi.org/10.3390/rs14133050
    - Supports the fire-source point: CNFDB polygons "often include unburned patches of
      forest and water bodies"; with NBAC, the NT1 range is "closer to 71%" undisturbed
      versus about 65% with agency perimeters. (Our CNFDB 2021-2024 rule exists for this
      reason.)

13. **Natural Resources Canada, Canadian Forest Service.** *Fire M3 Season-to-date Buffered
    Hotspots - Fire Perimeter Estimates* (CWFIS datamart, `downloads/hotspots/`; metadata
    PDF `fireM3_buffered_hotspots_metadata_NAP_ISO_19115_2003.pdf`).
    - Supports: perimeters "generated by combining and processing the season-to-date
      hotspots" (AVHRR, MODIS, VIIRS), updated daily; "should be considered as very rough
      estimates, and are best suited for large fires" (why we calibrate them against NBAC).

14. **Jain, P., Barber, Q.E., Taylor, S.W., et al. (2024).** Drivers and impacts of the
    record-breaking 2023 wildfire season in Canada. *Nature Communications*, 15, 6764.
    https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-51154-7 *(Crossref)*. "record-breaking total area
    burned (~15 Mha)".

15. **ECCC (2025-12-18).** *Canada's top 10 weather stories of 2025.* 2025 wildfires burned
    "roughly 8.9 million hectares"; "2025 ranked second only to the unprecedented wildfire
    season of 2023."
    **Public Safety Canada (2026-08-18).** 2026 season to date: "4,793 fires and 4.1 million
    hectares burned". (No official final 2026 total found as of 2026-11-17; Wikipedia's
    4.49 Mha from CIFFC 2026-09-29 is not a primary source.)

## Format (official, 2026-27 Classroom documents)

**Rules**
- Vancouver style: numbers in square brackets, [1], in citation order. The official
  Overleaf template's BibTeX setup handles this; keep it.
- 3,000-word hard limit. Abstract (250 words or fewer), references, tables, figures and
  captions are excluded; appendix text should be treated as counted.
- Title of 12 words or fewer; 3-5 keywords.
- AI may only copy-edit; all prose must be written by the students, and AI assistance
  with code or analysis should be disclosed.
